## Prompt

Create a photorealistic 16:9 landscape photograph in the style of a nature documentary: high drone shot with a
wide-angle lens, natural light, sharp detail, believable scale.

Setting: the planet Ares, real Mars terrain turned into an Earth-like world with oceans, NOT the red Mars of space
photos. The sky is blue with white clouds, the air is breathable, the sun looks the same size as from Earth and there
is no moon. Gravity is only 0.38 g, so life grows about 2.6× taller and lighter than on Earth: trees reach hundreds of
metres on huge buttresses, big animals walk slowly on long, very thin legs, and flyers have giant wingspans. The
planet is small, so the horizon is close. Rain falls in few but very heavy storms. All plants and animals are alien
but believable: no Earth species, no dinosaurs.

Scene: noon over a huge salt lake with no outlet, where a river dies inland. A braided river enters from the
foreground through green reed beds and salt-tolerant scrub, then spreads into vast, shallow brine that shades from
clear water at the inflow through bands of strong colour to crystallising white at the far end. Wide flats of cracked
white evaporite surround the water, marked with old shorelines like terraces stepping up the basin, each one a stand
still from a wetter year. Enormous flocks of long-winged flyers wade and feed in the shallows and rise in sheets over
the brine; nothing heavy stands far out on the soft salt. Beyond the flats the land is dry desert and low bare hills.
Blinding light, heavy heat shimmer distorting the far shore, a hazy sky with almost no cloud. Composition: the green
inflow in the foreground, the coloured brine bands in the middle, the white salt and shimmering horizon behind.

No people, buildings, roads, vehicles, spacecraft, text, labels, watermarks or borders. No red or orange sky. No Earth
animals.

## Variations

- Show the dry year: the lake gone, the whole basin a cracked white crust with only the river's channel scored across it.
- Show just after a cloudburst: a thin sheet of water covering the flats and mirroring the sky to the horizon.
