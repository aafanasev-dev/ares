## Prompt

Create a photorealistic 16:9 landscape photograph in the style of a nature documentary: wide-angle lens, natural
light, sharp detail, believable scale.

Setting: the planet Ares, real Mars terrain turned into an Earth-like world with oceans, NOT the red Mars of space
photos. The sky is blue with white clouds, the air is breathable, the sun looks the same size as from Earth and there
is no moon. Gravity is only 0.38 g, so life grows about 2.6× taller and lighter than on Earth: trees reach hundreds of
metres on huge buttresses, big animals walk slowly on long, very thin legs, and flyers have giant wingspans. The
planet is small, so the horizon is close. Rain falls in few but very heavy storms. All plants and animals are alien
but believable: no Earth species, no dinosaurs.

Scene: late afternoon on a cool, dry coast at about 50° south latitude. Rocky headlands formed by old crater rims jut
into a clear blue-green sea; a few rugged islands lie offshore, and long, slow swell breaks against the rocks. The land
is a cool desert: succulent scrub with swollen, water-storing stems, salt-tolerant strand plants along the beach,
gravel and bare rock, everything shaped by rare floods rather than steady rain. The air is extraordinarily clear, the
low sun casts long shadows and a light sea breeze blows. Far out at sea a single isolated cloudburst hangs as a dark
column of rain — one of only a few storms this coast will get all year. Composition: succulents and gravel in the
foreground, crater-rim headland and swell in the middle, islands and the distant cloudburst behind.

No people, buildings, roads, vehicles, spacecraft, text, labels, watermarks or borders. No red or orange sky. No Earth
animals.

## Variations

- Show a winter dawn: frost on the succulents and a pale, cold sky.
- Show the cloudburst reaching land: a flash flood cutting across the beach.
