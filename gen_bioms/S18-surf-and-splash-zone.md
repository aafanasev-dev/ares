## Prompt

Create a photorealistic 16:9 photograph in the style of a nature documentary: telephoto lens from a safe distance
along a rocky coast, natural light, sharp detail, believable scale.

Setting: the planet Ares, real Mars terrain turned into an Earth-like world with oceans, NOT the red Mars of space
photos. The sky is blue with white clouds, the air is breathable, the sun looks the same size as from Earth and there
is no moon. Gravity is only 0.38 g: ocean waves grow 1.6–2.6× taller and 2.6× longer than on Earth for the same wind
and roll in slowly with very long periods, spray hangs in the air, tides are tiny, and seabird-like flyers have giant
wingspans. The planet is small, so the horizon is close. All life is alien but believable: no Earth species.

Scene: an exposed rocky coast during a storm. Long, slow waves up to 25 m high arrive in widely spaced sets and slam
into the shore, throwing spray tens of metres up where it drifts slowly back down. Because the tides are feeble there
is almost no intertidal strip, but a very wide splash zone of wet black rock is permanently pounded by the surf. On
that rock live only low, thick, ferociously attached organisms: flattened crusts, domed shells and tough, rubbery mats
in green, rust and cream colours, packed into every crevice. Composition: close detail of attached organisms on wet
rock in the foreground, the pounded splash zone in the middle, a colossal breaking wave behind.

No people, boats, ships, buildings, vehicles, spacecraft, text, labels, watermarks or borders. No red or orange sky. No
Earth animals.
