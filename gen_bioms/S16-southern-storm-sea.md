## Prompt

Create a photorealistic 16:9 seascape photograph in the style of a nature documentary: telephoto lens from a coastal
cliff, dramatic natural light, sharp detail, believable scale.

Setting: the planet Ares, real Mars terrain turned into an Earth-like world with oceans, NOT the red Mars of space
photos. The sky is blue with white clouds, the air is breathable, the sun looks the same size as from Earth and there
is no moon. Gravity is only 0.38 g: ocean waves grow 1.6–2.6× taller and 2.6× longer than on Earth for the same wind
and roll in slowly with very long periods, spray hangs in the air, tides are tiny, and seabird-like flyers have giant
wingspans. The planet is small, so the horizon is close. All life is alien but believable: no Earth species.

Scene: a giant cyclone striking a forested southern coast at about 65° south. Westerly gale winds drive enormous storm
seas, 8–30 m high, onto the land; a storm surge about 25 m high has lifted the whole sea, which is pouring over low
ground and into the dark, wind-flattened forest behind the shore. Waves explode into towering clouds of slow-hanging
spray. The water is cold grey-green, streaked with foam, and the sky is a dark spiral of rain cloud. Giant flyers
struggle along the wind. Composition: surge water flooding over the shore and forest edge in the foreground, colossal
breaking waves in the middle, the dark storm sea and sky behind.

No people, boats, ships, buildings, vehicles, spacecraft, text, labels, watermarks or borders. No red or orange sky. No
Earth animals.

## Variations

- Show the open storm sea from low over the water, far from land, with giant waves under the cyclone.
- Show the calm after the storm: grey swell, flattened forest and debris along the shore.
