## Prompt

Create a photorealistic 16:9 seascape photograph in the style of a nature documentary: wide-angle lens low among the
waves, dramatic natural light, sharp detail, believable scale.

Setting: the planet Ares, real Mars terrain turned into an Earth-like world with oceans, NOT the red Mars of space
photos. The sky is blue with white clouds, the air is breathable, the sun looks the same size as from Earth and there
is no moon. Gravity is only 0.38 g: ocean waves grow 1.6–2.6× taller and 2.6× longer than on Earth for the same wind
and roll in slowly with very long periods, spray hangs in the air, tides are tiny, and seabird-like flyers have giant
wingspans. The planet is small, so the horizon is close. All life is alien but believable: no Earth species.

Scene: the open northern ocean at about 65° north inside one of the three giant cyclones of the hemisphere, with no
land anywhere to break it. Monstrous storm waves 22–35 m high, with a 48-second period, move slowly like moving
mountain ridges of dark grey-green water; their crests break and throw thick sheets of spray that hang in the air.
Westerly gale winds streak the wave faces with foam. The sky is a low, dark spiral of cloud with driving rain, and a
single gap lets a shaft of cold light fall on one wave. A giant flyer rides the wind along a wave face, dwarfed by it.
Composition: the face of a colossal wave in the foreground, the flyer for scale in the middle, more wave ridges and
storm cloud behind.

No people, boats, ships, buildings, vehicles, spacecraft, text, labels, watermarks or borders. No red or orange sky. No
Earth animals.

## Variations

- Show a calm spell between cyclones: long, smooth, grey swell under low cloud.
- Show summer near 70° north: storm waves lit by a low midnight sun.
